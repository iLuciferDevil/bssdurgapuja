Prologue · Mahalaya

When the darkness begins to break

Before sunrise on Mahalaya, a familiar invocation awakens Bengali homes. Pitri Paksha gives way to Devi Paksha. In Bengali imagination, this is when the air itself changes: Maa is on her way.

But the story we hear on Mahalaya begins before the homecoming. It begins with a world in danger, and with a demon who believes no power can stop him.

The celebrated Mahishasuramardini broadcast draws on the Devi Mahatmya and has shaped the Bengali experience of Mahalaya for generations.

Chapter I · The boon

Mahishasura rises

Mahishasura, the shape-shifting buffalo asura, wins a boon that makes him invulnerable to men and male gods. Certain that no woman could defeat him, he grows fearless.

He attacks the heavens, defeats Indra and the devas, and overturns the cosmic order. The gods who once ruled heaven are driven out. Their individual strength is not enough.

🐃
Chapter II · The birth of Shakti

From their light, She appears

Anger and radiance blaze from the gods and unite. From that concentrated tejas emerges a Goddess of overwhelming power: Durga, the collective Shakti that Mahishasura never imagined.

The gods arm her for battle. Shiva gives the trident. Vishnu gives the discus. Indra gives the thunderbolt. Vayu gives bow and arrows. The Himalaya gives her the lion. Many powers, one Goddess.

Chapter III · The Goddess rides to war

The roar that shakes the three worlds

Durga mounts her lion and confronts the armies of the asuras. The battle is not a single blow. Mahishasura's forces come in waves, and the Goddess answers with the weapons and powers gathered in her.

For the devotee, this warrior is also Maa. The same divine presence welcomed home during Puja is the force that restores balance when arrogance overwhelms the world.

The Puja story · Shashthi

Maa arrives at her maternal home

Alongside the battle narrative lives Bengal's beloved homecoming tradition. Durga is Uma, daughter of the house, returning from Kailash to her parents with Lakshmi, Saraswati, Ganesha and Kartikeya.

On Shashthi, Bodhon ceremonially awakens and invokes the Goddess. The face is unveiled. The guest we have waited for is finally home. Myth, family and worship become one story.

These are intertwined traditions rather than one literal chronological account: the Devi Mahatmya tells the cosmic battles; Bengali Durga Puja also imagines Durga's annual visit home.
🪔
Saptami · The battle intensifies

Every form meets its answer

Mahishasura is a master of changing form. Buffalo, lion, armed man, elephant: he transforms again and again, trying to overpower and confuse the Goddess. Durga meets every form without retreat.

In the Puja, Saptami begins with the ritual bathing and installation of the Nabapatrika. Outside the story, leaves, water, mantra and worship draw the community deeper into the presence of the Goddess.

Ashtami → Navami · Sandhi

108 lamps in the fiercest hour

At the sacred junction of Ashtami and Navami, Durga is worshipped in her fierce Chamunda aspect. A tradition associated with this moment remembers the destruction of Chanda and Munda. The Puja marks the threshold with 108 lamps and 108 lotuses.

The larger Devi Mahatmya also tells of Kali, Raktabija and the battles against Shumbha and Nishumbha. These belong to another episode of the scripture, not Mahishasura's battle, but Mahalaya's recitation and Shakta tradition place these many forms of Devi within one vast vision of Shakti.

This distinction matters: Chanda, Munda, Raktabija, Shumbha and Nishumbha are not Mahishasura's generals in the Devi Mahatmya. They appear in its later battle cycle.
🔥
Navami · The final confrontation

Mahishasuramardini

At last Mahishasura charges in his buffalo form. Durga pins him down. As he begins to emerge from that form, the Goddess strikes and kills him.

The seemingly impossible condition of his boon becomes his undoing. The power he dismissed is the power that defeats him. Durga becomes Mahishasuramardini: the slayer of Mahishasura.

Victory

Order returns

The devas praise the Goddess. The world breathes again. In the sacred story, Durga promises that whenever destructive forces rise again, the Divine will return in the form needed to confront them.

And in the pandal, Navami becomes precious precisely because everyone knows what comes next. The dhak is louder. The evening stretches. We try to hold on to one last full night with Maa.

🌺
Vijaya Dashami

The warrior becomes the daughter again

The battle is over. In Bengal's homecoming story, it is time for Uma to return to Kailash and Shiva. Baran is performed. Sindoor colours faces red. Pranam and embraces begin even before the farewell is complete.

Victory and separation arrive together. Vijaya remembers triumph, while Dashami carries the ache of a daughter leaving her maternal home.

🔴
Visarjan · Bijoya

She returns. The promise remains.

The image is carried to the water and immersed. Clay dissolves back into the elements from which it came. Maa returns to Kailash in the Bengali imagination, but Shakti does not leave the world.

Homes fill with sweets, embraces and Shubho Bijoya. Farewell becomes a promise repeated year after year.
