Prologue · Mahalaya

The dawn when Maa begins her journey

Before Puja begins, Bengal wakes in darkness. Mahalaya closes Pitri Paksha and opens Devi Paksha. The conch sounds. The Chandi is invoked. In Bengali imagination, Maa Durga begins the journey from Kailash to her earthly home.

But the voice of Mahalaya tells another story too: why the Mother had to become a warrior.

The iconic Mahishasuramardini tradition retells verses and episodes associated with the Devi Mahatmya. The homecoming of Uma from Kailash is a beloved Bengali layer of the festival tradition.

Chapter I · Mahishasura

The demon who believed a woman could never defeat him

Mahishasura was a powerful asura associated with the form of a buffalo. Through severe tapasya he won a boon: no man or male deity would kill him. The loophole seemed meaningless to him. What woman, he thought, could threaten his power?

That arrogance changed everything. Mahishasura attacked the devas, defeated their armies and seized Svarga. Indra and the gods were driven from heaven. Cosmic order gave way to fear.

🐃
Chapter II · The devas seek help

No god could defeat him alone

The defeated devas went to Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva. Their anger became radiance. Light blazed from the gods, converging until it became one unbearable brilliance.

From that union of divine energy emerged a woman. Not a lesser power, but Shakti itself: Durga. The answer to Mahishasura's boon was the power he had dismissed.

Chapter III · Durga is armed

Many gods. One Shakti.

The gods placed their powers in her hands. Shiva gave his trident. Vishnu his discus. Indra the thunderbolt. Vayu the bow and arrows. Varuna the conch. Agni the spear. Other deities offered sword, bell, armour and gifts. Himavan gave her a lion.

She was beautiful and terrifying, serene and unstoppable. When Durga roared, the worlds trembled.

Interlude · The family Bengal welcomes home

Warrior. Mother. Daughter.

Bengal tells the cosmic story through the intimacy of a family homecoming. Durga is also Uma, daughter of the Himalaya and Menaka, wife of Shiva, returning from Kailash for a few precious days.

In the familiar Bengali Durga tableau she arrives with Lakshmi, Saraswati, Ganesha and Kartikeya. Shiva remains in Kailash. The divine warrior suddenly feels like someone from our own family who has come home after a long year.

Scriptural genealogies and regional traditions differ. The Bengali Puja tableau conventionally presents Lakshmi, Saraswati, Ganesha and Kartikeya as Durga's children and Shiva as her husband. This storybook follows that Bengali festival imagination while keeping the Mahishasura narrative distinct.

Shashthi · Bodhon

The curtain opens. Maa is here.

By Shashthi, the homecoming becomes presence. Bodhon invokes and awakens the Goddess for worship. The face is revealed, conch shells sound and the pandal changes from a structure into a sacred home.

In the story unfolding behind the ritual, the warrior Goddess has already accepted the challenge. She rides toward Mahishasura's armies on her lion.

Saptami · The battle expands

Mahishasura changes form

Mahishasura's strength is not only brute force. He changes shape. Buffalo becomes lion. Lion becomes armed man. Man becomes elephant. Each transformation is another attempt to escape the Goddess.

Durga answers every form. When he becomes a lion, she moves to strike its head. When he becomes an elephant and seizes her lion, she cuts the trunk. He returns again to the buffalo form, raging across the battlefield.

Meanwhile, Saptami worship begins with the Nabapatrika, ritually bathed and installed. Nature itself is drawn into the worship of Shakti.

Ashtami · Shakti at full force

The Goddess does not tire

Ashtami is the emotional centre of Durga Puja. Devotees gather for Pushpanjali while the mythology remembers the Goddess in her most powerful forms. The contrast is striking: flowers in human hands, weapons in the hands of Devi.

Durga's power is not rage without purpose. It is force used to restore dharma. Her many arms become a visual language for many powers acting at once: protection, knowledge, courage, discipline and destruction of what threatens balance.

Sandhi · Ashtami meets Navami

The fiercest threshold

Sandhi Puja occupies the sacred junction between Ashtami and Navami. In Bengal it is one of the most intense moments of the festival, traditionally marked by 108 lamps and 108 lotuses.

It is associated in Puja tradition with Devi's fierce Chamunda form and the destruction of Chanda and Munda. The Devi Mahatmya places Chanda and Munda, Raktabija, Shumbha and Nishumbha in a later battle cycle than Mahishasura. They are different episodes in the scripture, even though Bengali worship holds all these manifestations within the larger story of the Goddess.

Navami · Mahishasura Mardini

The final form. The final strike.

The battle reaches Mahishasura himself. In buffalo form he tears through the earth, hurls mountains and charges the Goddess. Durga binds him. He tries to transform again.

She pins the buffalo beneath her foot. As the asura begins to emerge from the animal form, she strikes. The trident and blade end the battle. The being who believed no woman could defeat him falls before the Goddess born from the combined power of the gods.

Mahishasura is defeated. Durga becomes Mahishasuramardini, the slayer of Mahishasura.

Vijaya Dashami · Victory and farewell

The warrior becomes the daughter again

Victory does not end the Bengali story. On Dashami, the mood changes. The battle is over. The Goddess who restored the worlds is once again Maa, the daughter preparing to leave her earthly home.

Women offer baran and sindoor. Families bow for pranam. There is celebration because Devi has won, and sadness because the few days of homecoming are ending.

Vijaya means victory, but emotionally it is also farewell.

Visarjan · The return to Kailash

She leaves. She never really leaves.

The image of Durga is carried toward the water amid dhaak, chants and tears. Immersion returns the clay to the elements. In the homecoming tradition, Maa begins her journey back to Shiva in Kailash with her children.

Then comes Bijoya: embraces, sweets, elders' blessings and the sentence that turns an ending into a promise.

Asche bochor abar hobe.

Next year, it will happen again. The Mother will return. The story will be retold. And for a few days, wherever Bengalis have made a home, Maa will come home too.
