What makes a BSS Pujo feel Sylheti?

It is difficult to explain only through a programme list. You notice it in the conversations around you, in familiar food and music, and especially when Dhamail begins. Dhamail is a folk song-and-dance tradition closely associated with the Sylhet region. Traditionally, women move in a circle, singing and keeping rhythm through clapping. At BSS, our Dhamail of Unity is part of Maha Navami on 20 October.

That Sylheti character sits comfortably beside the Durga Puja traditions familiar across Bengal and the Bengali diaspora: Pushpanjali, Sandhya Arati, dhak, Dhunuchi, Alpona, Sindhur Khela, music and community meals. Durga Puja itself has travelled far beyond Bengal; UNESCO describes the festival as a major public expression of religion, art and collective participation, and notes how crowds across communities gather around it.

Why do we celebrate Durga Puja?

At the heart of the festival is the story of Goddess Durga's victory over Mahishasura. The story is told in the Devi Mahatmya, an important text in the Shakta tradition. For generations, the victory has been understood as the triumph of divine power over destructive forces.

But in Bengali culture, Durga Puja also carries a wonderfully human emotion: Maa is coming home. Durga is welcomed as a daughter returning to her maternal home with her children. That is why the festival can feel devotional and deeply familial at the same time. We welcome her, spend a few precious days together, and on Bijoya Dashami say goodbye until next year.

From Mahalaya to Bijoya: what the days mean

MahalayaThe emotional beginning of Devi Paksha and the anticipation of Maa's arrival.
ShashthiThe main Puja days begin, with Bodhon and the ceremonial welcome of the Goddess.
SaptamiNabapatrika and the morning rituals mark the day.
AshtamiOne of the most important days for Pushpanjali and worship.
NavamiThe final full day of worship, and at BSS, a day filled with community celebration.
Bijoya DashamiA farewell to Maa Durga, followed by greetings, embraces and the promise of asche bochor abar hobe.
