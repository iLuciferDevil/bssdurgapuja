What is a Sylheti Durga Puja?

A Sylheti Durga Puja combines the worship and shared traditions of Durga Puja with the language, music, memories and community life of people connected to the Sylhet region. At BSS, that identity is most visible in the conversations around the venue and in cultural expressions such as Dhamail.

The Puja is still familiar to anyone who has attended a Bengali Durga Puja: there is Pushpanjali, Bhog, Sandhya Arati, Dhunuchi, Sindhur Khela, music and time spent together. The Sylheti character adds another layer to that shared Bengali festival experience.

BSS does not present the celebration as a closed community gathering. The organisation welcomes Sylhetis, Bengalis, non-Bengalis and first-time visitors. If you are curious about the rituals or cultural programme, you can attend and ask the team for guidance.

What makes the BSS celebration distinctly Sylheti?

Dhamail of UnityDhamail is a folk song-and-dance tradition associated with Sylheti culture. BSS has confirmed Dhamail of Unity for 12:00 PM on Tuesday, 20 October.
Language and belongingSylheti speech, familiar expressions and conversations help Bengaluru feel closer to home for community members during Pujo.
Community across generationsThe confirmed programme includes children’s activities, music, creative events and traditional observances rather than treating Puja as only a ritual timetable.
An open invitationVisitors do not need to be members of the Sylheti or Bengali community to experience the Puja and cultural programme.

First time at a Bengali or Sylheti Durga Puja?

You can choose a short visit for one programme or spend more of the day at the venue. Morning visitors can plan around Puja or Pushpanjali. Afternoon visitors can plan around Bhog. Evening visitors can choose cultural programmes such as live music, Dandiya or Dhunuchi.

There is no need to pretend that you already know every custom. Observe respectfully, follow instructions from the organisers around worship areas and ask if you are unsure. BSS’s published welcome applies to everyone, not only Sylhetis or Bengalis.
