From Mahalaya to Bijoya: what the days of Durga Puja mean

A beginner-friendly guide to the names, emotions and traditions—connected to the confirmed BSS Durga Puja programme in Hoodi from 16 to 21 October 2026.

The Durga Puja days in one minute

Mahalaya begins the emotional and devotional countdown. Shashthi welcomes Maa Durga; Saptami begins the principal worship; Ashtami carries some of the festival's most significant observances; Navami is the last full day of worship; and Bijoya Dashami is the farewell followed by greetings and reunion.

At BSS in Bengaluru, the confirmed 2026 celebration runs from Friday, 16 October through Wednesday, 21 October at The Occasion, Hoodi.

Before the main community Puja days

Mahalaya: the wait becomes real

Mahalaya is associated with the beginning of Devi Paksha and the invocation of the Goddess in popular Bengali tradition. For many families, the early-morning sound of Chandipath is the moment when Pujo stops feeling distant.

Mahalaya and Shashthi are not the same event. Mahalaya sets the devotional and emotional context; the main BSS community celebration begins with Maha Shasthi on 16 October.

Friday · 16 October 2026

Shashthi: welcoming Maa Durga

Shashthi marks the start of the main celebration. Bodhon, Amantran and Adhibas are welcoming rituals associated with formally beginning the worship of the Goddess.

At BSS, Shashthi Puja is at 6:06 AM and Pushpanjali is at 9:30 AM. Bhog is served from 1:30 PM to 3:30 PM. Bodhon, Amantran and Adhibas are listed at 7:00 PM, followed by Agomoni and children's performances.

Saturday · 17 October 2026

Saptami: Nabapatrika and the Puja gathers pace

Saptami is commonly associated with the Nabapatrika ritual, involving nine plants that are bound together, ritually bathed and placed within the worship. Practices and explanations can vary across traditions.

The BSS programme lists Nabo Patrika Sthapan at 7:30 AM, Saptami Puja at 8:30 AM and Pushpanjali at 11:00 AM. Bhog is served from 1:30 PM to 3:30 PM, followed later by Sandhya Arati and the Ochin Pakhi live band.

Sunday–Monday · 18–19 October 2026

Ashtami: Pushpanjali, Sandhi Puja and the festival's devotional centre

Ashtami is one of the most significant stages of Durga Puja. Pushpanjali is central for many visitors, while Sandhi Puja marks the traditional 48-minute junction between Ashtami and Navami.

BSS lists Pushpanjali at 11:00 AM on both 18 and 19 October. On 19 October, Sandhi Puja is confirmed at 7:26 AM. The evening programmes differ: DJ & Dandiya on 18 October, and Dhaker Tale with the Dhunuchi Competition on 19 October.

Use the BSS Ashtami visitor guide for the two-day plan and the Sandhi Puja explainer for the meaning of the ritual.

Tuesday · 20 October 2026

Navami: the last full day

Navami continues the worship while carrying the feeling that the farewell is approaching. At BSS, it is also the day when Sylheti cultural identity is especially visible through Dhamail.

Navami Puja is at 6:00 AM and Navami Yagya at 7:30 AM. Pushpanjali is at 11:00 AM, followed by Dhamail of Unity at 12:00 PM, Bhog from 1:30 PM to 3:30 PM and Dandiya at 7:30 PM.

See the BSS Navami visitor guide for the complete confirmed programme.

Wednesday · 21 October 2026

Bijoya Dashami: farewell, then reconnection

Dashami marks the farewell to Maa Durga. Sindhur Khela is one of the customs closely associated with the day in Bengali community celebrations. After the farewell comes Bijoya: pronam to elders, greetings, embraces, sweets and reconnecting with people.

The BSS programme lists Dashami Puja at 6:00 AM, Sindhur Khela at 10:30 AM, Bhog from 1:30 PM to 3:30 PM and Shanti Jol at 7:00 PM. Participation rules for Sindhur Khela have not been published, so visitors should follow the organising team's guidance.

The Bijoya Dashami and Sindhur Khela guide contains the focused visitor information.

Frequently asked questions

What are the main days of Durga Puja?

The familiar sequence begins with Mahalaya and continues through Shashthi, Saptami, Ashtami, Navami and Bijoya Dashami.

When is BSS Durga Puja in Bangalore in 2026?

BSS Durga Puja runs from Friday, 16 October through Wednesday, 21 October at The Occasion, Hoodi.

What is the difference between Mahalaya and Shashthi?

Mahalaya sets the devotional and emotional context through the invocation of the Goddess. Shashthi begins the main community celebration and welcoming rituals.

Why does the BSS schedule show two Ashtami programme days?

The programme shared by the BSS organising team labels both 18 and 19 October as Ashtami programme days. BSS pages retain that wording.

What does Bijoya mean after Dashami?

Bijoya is the period of greetings and reconnection after the farewell to Maa Durga, commonly associated with pronam, embraces and sweets.

Can first-time and non-Bengali visitors attend?

Yes. Everyone is welcome at BSS Durga Puja. Use the first-time visitor guide for the practical details currently confirmed by BSS.
