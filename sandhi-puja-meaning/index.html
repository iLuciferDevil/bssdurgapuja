What is Sandhi Puja? Meaning, tradition and BSS 2026 time

Understand the 48-minute junction between Ashtami and Navami, the story of Chamunda, and when to experience the confirmed BSS observance in Hoodi.

Sandhi Puja in one minute

Sandhi Puja is observed at the junction of Ashtami and Navami. Traditionally, the sandhi lasts 48 minutes: the final 24 minutes of Ashtami and the first 24 minutes of Navami. The observance is associated with Maa Durga's fierce Chamunda form and the defeat of Chanda and Munda.

At BSS Durga Puja 2026, Sandhi Puja is listed at 7:26 AM on Monday, 19 October, at The Occasion, Hoodi, Bengaluru.

Why does Sandhi Puja last 48 traditional minutes?

The word sandhi refers to a junction or transition. In this context, it is the meeting point of two tithis: Ashtami and Navami. The traditional observance covers the last 24 minutes of Ashtami and the first 24 minutes of Navami.

That is why Sandhi Puja does not have one fixed clock time every year. Its timing follows the ritual calendar and may fall in the morning, afternoon, evening or night. For BSS in 2026, the organising team's published programme lists 7:26 AM on 19 October.

Timing note: BSS has published a 7:26 AM start time but has not published a separate end time. This page does not calculate or present an inferred BSS end time.

The story of Durga, Chanda and Munda

Sandhi Puja is traditionally connected with the episode in which the Goddess confronts Chanda and Munda. Durga manifests a fierce form, Chamunda, who defeats them. The name Chamunda is commonly understood through this victory over Chanda and Munda.

The ritual therefore marks more than a transition between two calendar days. It recalls a decisive moment of transformation and the power of the Goddess confronting destructive forces.

Why are 108 lamps and lotuses associated with Sandhi Puja?

Many Durga Puja traditions associate Sandhi Puja with offerings such as 108 lamps and 108 lotuses. Practices can differ between communities, organising committees and years.

The current BSS 2026 information confirms the Sandhi Puja time, but it does not publish a list of ritual materials or state whether visitors should bring flowers, lamps or other offerings. Visitors should follow the priest's and organising team's guidance at the venue.
